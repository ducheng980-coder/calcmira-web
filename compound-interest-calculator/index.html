Compound Interest Calculator

See how savings grow when interest earns interest. Enter a starting amount, annual rate, years, compounding frequency and an optional monthly contribution to get the future value and how much of it is interest.

How to use the Compound Interest Calculator

  1. Enter the starting amount and annual interest rate.
  2. Enter the number of years and compounding frequency.
  3. Optionally add a monthly contribution, then calculate.

Formula and method

Future value of the principal = P × (1 + r/f)^(f×t). Contributions are accumulated monthly using the equivalent monthly rate. The effective annual rate is (1 + r/f)^f − 1.

Example

$10,000 at 5% for 10 years, compounded monthly, grows to about $16,470 — $6,470 of it interest, before any contributions. Use the “Load example” button above to try it with the sample inputs.

Frequently asked questions

Does this account for inflation or tax?

No. It shows nominal growth before tax and inflation. Real (inflation-adjusted) returns are lower.

Why does compounding frequency matter?

More frequent compounding means interest starts earning interest sooner. The effect is real but modest at typical savings rates.
